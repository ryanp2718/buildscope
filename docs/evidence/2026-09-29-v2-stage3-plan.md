## Purpose

Stage 3 buys the top tier: five models, the most expensive and the slowest of the run. It has to fit
what is left of the cap, finish while one connection stays up, and, if either runs short, lose
the draws the pre-registration says to lose first. Stage 2 showed that the harness's spend limits do
not do that on their own. This fixes how stage 3 is sized, ordered and routed.

## What stage 2 showed about spend limits

`--max-spend` is a ceiling per process, and the harness checks it before every call against that
call's **worst case**, the full output cap at the model's price. It also charges a call that failed
after the host started answering at its worst case, since the host may bill for tokens nobody
reported. Both keep the ceiling a guarantee. In stage 2 they stopped three processes that had not
spent their limit:

| process | limit | stopped at | draws actually cost | cause |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| GPT-6 Sol | $1.50 | $0.43 | $0.43 | next call's worst case $1.09 |
| MiniMax M3 | $0.57 | $0.39 | $0.39 | a re-draw at 128,000 tokens, worst case $0.23 |
| Gemini 3.8 Flash | $3.90 | $3.89 | about $2.04 | five failed calls charged $0.37 each |

Each was relaunched for its unfinished cells, and every stage 2 cell completed. (MiMo V2.6 Pro's
process was also stopped once, by the machine running out of memory rather than by its limit, and
relaunched the same way.) Stage 2 cost $7.85 against the $8.29 typical projection after cut 1.
Santa Barbara, which the pre-registration never cuts, ran last in every process, so it was the cell
the stops reached first.

So a process needs headroom: its limit must be at least its expected spend plus one worst-case call
(w), or it stops before finishing even at typical cost. Failed calls charged at worst case need
more. Five processes each hold their own headroom.

## Fitting the cap

The pre-registration tests the cap against the typical projection. On that test stage 3 fit $45
with no further cut: $19.25 + $22.62 + $0.79 is $42.66. But limits sized to typical cost stop a
model before it finishes, as stage 2 showed, so the test that matters is whether every process can
be given its typical cost plus one worst-case call:

| cuts applied | whole run, typical | sum of typical + w | fits $45 (room $25.75) | fits $50 (room $30.75) |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| cut 1 only (as now), glm-5.2's hint cell run | $42.66 | $28.81 | no | **yes, $1.94 spare** |
| 2. the v1 re-runs of kimi-k2-thinking and glm-5.2 (drops glm-5.2's hint cell) | $41.87 | $27.53 | no | yes |
| 3. St. Johns for the top tier, 10 draws to 5 | $37.83 | $23.49 | yes | yes |
| 4. Clark for the top tier stops at 10 | $34.75 | $20.41 | yes | yes |

**Decided 2026-09-29 (amendment 3):** under $45 this needed cuts 2 and 3. The user raised the cap
to $50 rather than cut cells, so stage 3 runs every pre-registered cell, and glm-5.2's hint cell
runs. The stricter reading of "fits" is kept: cut 4 applies only if a later re-projection puts the
sum of typical plus w over what is left.

**The $1.94 spare.** Given to the processes up front, in proportion to how far each model's heavy
case sits above its typical (grok-4.7 and qwen3.8-max-0902 get most), so that a model running heavy
loses fewer St. Johns and second-stage Clark draws before the time box, not after a relaunch. The
limits then sum to the room less a small margin, and the cap stays a guarantee. Splitting a slow
model over two processes (below) takes its extra headroom out of the spare first.

## Cell order: overruns cut what the pre-registration cuts first

A process that runs heavy, or runs out of time, stops wherever it is. So each process lists its
cells in the order the pre-registration protects them: the never-cut cells first, then what cut 4
removes last, then what cut 3 removes first.

    clarkco:M:10, santabarbara:M:5, stjohns:M:5, clarkco:M:10+10, stjohns:M:10

The later entries repeat a cell with more draws: the draws already bought replay from the cache at
no cost, and the cell continues. The Clark cell still stops at 10 if the first 10 agree. A model
that runs heavy then loses its last St. Johns draws
first, then its second 10 Clark draws, and never its first 10 Clark draws or Santa Barbara. Every
draw lost this way is reported per model as cut by the ceiling.

A cell written as `clarkco:M:10` and then continued as `clarkco:M:10+10` is recorded as one
two-stage cell (checked in the code; see "Processes and time").

## Routing for glm-5.3

Exclude the five hosts that failed glm-5.3-flash, as stage 1's proposal 4 set out: AtlasCloud and
Phala (streams closed at 301 s and 602 s), GMICloud (602 s) and Io Net (1,800 s), and SiliconFlow,
which closed one stream at 219 s and stopped two at 32,768 tokens below the requested cap, giving
those draws a second attempt other draws did not get. It also completed four calls past 32,768, so
its stop is not a fixed limit; the exclusion rests on the unequal second attempt, not on a known
limit. glm-5.3's listing names 40 endpoints, but its precision filter (fp8 or better) and the
parameters the request requires leave 12, and the exclusions take 4 of those (Phala's endpoint is
already filtered out, as it reports no precision). 8 remain: AkashML, Baidu, Morph, Novita, Parasail,
Sail Research (two endpoints) and Z.ai itself. The exclusion is in its `ModelSpec.ignore`, and
`tests/test_models.py` checks the slugs against the endpoint listing and the 8 that remain.

Routing is part of the request, so the smoke draw misses the cache and is re-bought, about $0.17,
and the earlier one is superseded, as deviation 2 did for glm-5.3-flash. The evidence is from the
flash model; a host could limit one deployment and not another.

## What this does not establish

- **The figures.** The top-tier projections rest on one draw per model scaled by a ratio measured
  on cheaper models, and w on registry prices at the 64,000 cap.
- **That per-model ceilings treat the top tier alike.** A model that runs heavy loses St. Johns draws
  that a model at typical cost keeps, so n on St. Johns can differ across the tier. The pre-registered
  cuts were tier-wide. The spare, given in proportion to each model's heavy case, makes this less
  likely; the cell order only governs what an overrun takes.
- **Run times.** They scale from one smoke draw per model. grok-4.7's ran past the output cap
  (deviation 1), and qwen3.8-max-0902 has one host, so either could run slower than shown.
- **That the glm-5.3 exclusions help.** They are inferred from glm-5.3-flash. No glm-5.3 call has
  been cut yet; its one call ran on Sail Research.
